“Would anybody be able to tell it was YOU spinning?”

What got you hooked on house?

So, actually I was so young when I got hooked on house it wasn't a party that did it. I was 14 or 15 yrs old, no DL yet, and both sides of my family are from Arkansas so I didn't have older cousins or Aunts or Uncles in the city of Chicago. I grew up in the burbs so what got me hooked was the mixes on WGCI and WBMX that's what hooked me. I used to go to bed around 10 or 11 and put my headphones on and listen all the way til 5am. An hour of commercial free mixes one DJ after another! I would switch back and forth between the stations to see which DJ was playing the tracks I liked best. The first house record I bought was Inner City – Out of Control. THAT BASSLINE!! Might not blow people away in nowadays but in 1986... man.

Who inspired you to get on the tables?

I really loved the mixes and the music so much that I honestly kind of liked everybody that was on those stations BUT without question my favorite DJ and the man that inspired me to want to become a DJ was Farley “Funkin'” Keith bka Farley “Jackmaster” Funk! My 2nd favorite was Mike “Hitman” Wilson! And I'd like to give honorable mention to Bad Boy Bill, Ralphie Rosario and Kenny “Jammin” Jason. If you're an Acidman fan 1st and foremost “thank you <3” but you have to thank those guys who came before me and inspired me to get on the tables. But the people I learned from the most (by listening and also being on the floor dancing) are definitely Farley, Ralphie, Spencer Kincy and Derrick Carter who in my humble but correct, irrefutable opinion is the MJ / Muhammad Ali of house DJ's. Some people are on BS but I believe in giving credit where it is due. And I wanna give a shout out to Miles Maeda who is another influential DJ / friend who doesn't get enough credit as a DJ.

Where did the name come from?

So, before I started mixing I wanted to be a rapper (lol) so I bought a Casio drum machine. I could write rhymes but I felt my voice sucked and I didn't really have the confidence to rap them. So after I got hooked on house music I traded my Casio for 1200's to the man who taught me how to spin, “Iceman” Ivan Bey. Ivan was giving up DJing to become a Christian rapper so it's kinda like we traded places. So in the 80's everybody had a moniker: “Bad Boy Bill”, “Jumpin'” Julian Perez, Ralphie “the Razz” Rosario. So Ivan and a couple of other DJ's in the crew (in my bedroom) said you need a DJ name and we agreed I needed something to go with the letter “A.” So what was the brand new thing at that time? ACID HOUSE. So we fumbled around with ideas for a minute and “Acid Al” was my original DJ name. BUT I've always been a fan of comic books and superheroes so in 1990 I believe it was I changed it to “Acidman” because I felt “Acid Al” was too “80's” and I felt Acidman was more timeless just like Superman, or Spiderman, they've stood the test of time. Yes I love acid house (who doesn't) but I've always been misunderstood. I've never taken acid or any drugs and I am a HOUSE DJ not just an acid house DJ. I don't know anybody who was back in those days, we just all played house. The only separation I knew of in the early 90's was some DJ's being known for playing deep house. All these sub genre specific DJ's came wayyy later to the best of my knowledge.

How did Shadefree come together?

This question is awesome and kinda makes me laugh a little bit. So one of my best friends Gary was a long time party promoter of old school parties at VFW halls, banquet rooms and what not. I gotta give him props because to the best of my recollection he was the 1st one to ever officially book me for a party way back before the rave scene started. He even had parties at a train station in West Chicago called the Underground. I played lots of parties for him along with a guy you may have heard of called DJ Traxx. I got into the rave scene pretty early but for whatever reason the rave promoters weren't booking me to play. I was actually a little salty about that truth be told, I was even part of the original crew for Pure. So this is the funny part: remember I wasn't getting to play raves, I had been telling Gary he needed to forget about doing those old school parties because rave parties were where it was at now. For a long time he refused saying he wanted to stick with what he knew. Then one night this dude I knew threw a HUGE party at the Harvey Expo Center, I wanna say the party was called “Voodoo”, and because we were buddies and I'm an upstanding, shiny beacon on a hill of a guy (lol jk) this buddy of mine had me and his right hand man do a money drop, meaning we took lawn bags of cash from the party all the way downtown Chicago to his family (I think.) I shudder to think what would've happened if I had gotten pulled over. So one night at Gary and I's meeting place (Denny's) I told him about Voodoo and the cash drop. It was at that moment Gary got real interested in throwing a rave lol. So since I was in the scene and familiar we started talking about doing our own production and I wrote down a list of about 15 or so names. At that time all the ravers were calling every promoter “shady.” So one of the names I came up with on that list was “Shadefree” and as we discussed the list we agreed that was the best one. If other promoters that were 1st had booked me, Gary agrees Shadefree probably never would've existed. As for what it was like being the resident... it was awesome, I finally had parties to show what I could do and get noticed by other promoters.

Vinyl or digital?

I have contemplated switching to digital for a long time. I see the merits of it at times especially when you have to move lol. But I grew with vinyl, I love playing vinyl, imperfect and less convenient though it may be. I am an artist, I love it when the EP has a jacket with artwork. I miss walking into Gramaphone (which is still alive and well) and seeing all the DJ's working there: Derrick, Mark, Miles, Oscar, etc. And I miss all the other DJ's and friends you would see in there while you were shopping. It was like a watering hole in the plains of Africa, everybody would gather there and you never knew what out of town DJ or performer might roll through. Also, vinyl imo weeds out the phonies for lack of a better term. It requires more: more skill, more money (that's not great) but if we didn't have digital I don't believe there would be quite as much over saturation as there is today. I could be wrong, maybe it's the internet's fault. I just feel like it takes a little more love and a little dedication to your craft than the sync button. There are DJ's who grew up spinning vinyl and now play digital, they know who they are and they get a pass because they've been around and if they had to play vinyl they could still beat it down. But if y'all wanna do a gofundme and raise the money to buy me a pair of CDJ 2 or 3000's I'll take the leap because it is frustrating to hear a track you really like and it's not pressed on vinyl. That said the older shit is better.

A night you'll never forget?

This is a tough question. I'm gonna be 55 on Saturday (Halloween) and I'm not being funny when I say I've been having memory problems lately. There's so many nights good and bad it's hard to remember any one thing. I could do a podcast (and I think I'm gonna) about various nights. Okay, I remember being on the floor dancing my ass off to Ralphie Rosario at the Shelter, the floor was packed and there was an Asian guy in a full suit with a tie and everything but his tie and collar were loosened because he was sweating his ass off. I looked over at him and over the loud thumping music I heard him yell “I thought I hated house music! I was a FOOLLLL!!!” I'll never forget that. I have other such instances but I'll leave it at that.

Sharing bills with the names you grew up on?

This is an easy question. An honor. Those guys and so many more are DJ's / producers you love and respect and I think I speak for everybody when I say I miss Paul, he was everybody's friend and an unbelievable talent. I miss Green Velvet too. I still call him Cajmere. Yes he's alive and well, but doing so well that I don't ever see him around anymore because I'm not big on festivals, except West Fest.

Lima and San Francisco: how did it go?

I've been honest with my answers and I'll be honest again. Good, but not as good as I would have liked. Both were learning experiences. Lima, some of the people came up and told me they loved the set because I think I was the 1st international DJ they had there or maybe just from the states. But some of the people expected / wanted me to play much harder, which I could have if I had known that's what they wanted, but I got booked because the promoter heard one of my mix tapes so I thought that's what they wanted. SF, the 1st time I feel didn't give me a fair chance. Things started off great while I was playing housey, groovy shit, then I played a couple of acid tracks and I lost the floor! Hello! You came out to hear “Acidman” and it was my 1st set there so I guess they didn't know that 2 or 3 tracks later I went right back to groovy housey shit. I don't stay in one place during my sets. But there were a couple people REALLY loving it and they came up and said thank you after I “dropped some acid” lol. The 2nd time in SF I was in pain and sick as a dog but the crowd stayed all nite so it went way better even though I thought I played better the 1st time. Chicago can be hit or miss but I love Chicago cuz I'm from here and you know more people here. SF had a great vibe though and in some ways reminded me of Milwaukee.

Chicago then and now?

It has changed. The rave days were magical because it was the 90's and every single week there were just way more options. The clubs were jumping off even if you didn't have a big out of town headliner: Shelter, Crobar, Red Dog, China Club, the Riviera, Medusa's and more. So you had a jumping club scene, a jumping rave scene and then the loft parties were going off as well. You could go out 5, 6 nights a week if you wanted to and find a decent party on almost every night. Now it feels like there's only a couple of spots by comparison, not much rave wise even though some younger folks are still trying to do stuff. But you know, no more flyers, no more check points, no more club kids except Queen, no more real “atmosphere” in the undergrounds I feel like. In fairness I'm not out anywhere near as much these days.

A record you play more than you should, and advice to a young DJ?

More honesty. There are tons of tracks I like more BUT, I don't play a ton of vocal tracks so I have a house remix of Michael Jackson's “Off the Wall” that I play out more often than I should but it's a fun record to play when I'm trying to throw the crowd a bone because if you're over 40 and not a DJ you probably want some vocals. Advice to a young DJ? Don't! We don't need any more DJ's, there's too many already. But if you feel you must: spend time on the dance floor 1st! Also it's an advantage if you are familiar with the venue and the crowd. And most importantly bring something new to the table! Almost all DJ's think they're the shizzle Mcdizzle for rizzle. Okay, ask yourself if you were mixing and nobody could see you and there were no name drops saying “DJ so and so in the mix!” would anybody be able to tell it was YOU spinning? If not, take off the headphones and get back on the fucking floor and dance! :)
